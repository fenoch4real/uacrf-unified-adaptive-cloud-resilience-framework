# Internal contracts and state machines

Version 1.0 | Architecture revision MS 01

These are target contracts for implementation. They supplement the existing public wire contracts and are not counted as completed handlers. Calls require authenticated service identity, a receiving audience and the original actor's narrowed scope. Owner responses are authoritative only for their named resource, revision, purpose and validity interval.

## Owner receipt protocol

An owner receipt contains `key_id`, `receipt_id`, `issuer_service`, `audience_service`, `tenant_id`, `environment_id`, `actor_id`, `resource_type`, `resource_id`, `resource_revision`, `content_digest`, `authority_epoch`, `eligible`, `purpose`, `issued_at`, `valid_until` and a detached signature. Versioned resources also carry `resource_version`, which is required for `agent_workload` and `service` receipts. Its schema is in `contracts/owner-receipt.schema.json`. The body is signed under the owner's registered key and verified against an allowlisted issuer/audience/algorithm and current trust configuration. A client-provided verified/eligible flag is never accepted instead of an owner-issued response.

Current access and content permission are separate from historical authenticity. A valid old signature proves retained attribution, not continued approval. Mutating decisions ask for fresh receipts and compare the control fence. No receipt extends beyond the source owner's eligibility deadline or the requesting operation's deadline.

### S04-qualified agent-workload dependency proposals

The first evidence-backed S06 slice supports only `agent_workload DEPENDS_ON agent_workload`. Other endpoint types and relationship types are rejected by this workflow; this is a bounded first slice, not a declaration that the other graph domains are complete. A proposal is authored as typed endpoints by a human and remains `PROPOSED` until a distinct S06 reviewer accepts or rejects it. A qualified S04 observation is supporting evidence, not proof of endpoint ownership and not an instruction to create an edge.

S06 obtains owner proof separately for each endpoint from S02 and a qualification summary from S04. For proposal creation and review, the signed S02 receipt's `actor_id` is the original human proposer or reviewer. S06 forwards its current S01-signed S06-audience delegation to S01's resource-delegation exchange. S01 verifies that assertion, resolves its original actor to the current identity directory, rechecks the relevant `topology.proposal.write` or `topology.proposal.review` membership, and issues a single-resource, single-purpose delegation. The exchange accepts no caller-supplied actor ID or downstream capability. For current-use rechecks, `actor_id` is the current authenticated graph-receipt caller (which may be an authorized service principal), and S01 rechecks `topology.graph.read`.

The exchange is `POST /internal/v1/delegations:exchange`, authenticated by the S01-signed token whose audience is S06. Its body contains only `audience`, `resource_type`, `resource_id`, `purpose`, and `operation_deadline`. S01 derives the downstream capability from a closed mapping: S02 plus `agent_workload` yields `catalog.agent.owner-receipt`; S02 plus `service` yields `catalog.service.read`; S04 plus `evidence_event` yields `evidence.topology.summary`; and S07 plus `assessment_case` yields `assessment.case.read`. Purpose is one of `topology.dependency-proposal`, `topology.dependency-review`, `topology.graph.current-use`, `topology.graph.subgraph-selection`, or `topology.graph.difference-classification`; S01 checks that the caller has the matching topology capability. The resulting delegation has the resource ID as its service scope, repeats the exact purpose/resource claims, and expires no later than the earlier of five seconds after issuance and `operation_deadline`. S01 rejects an operation deadline more than five seconds in the future or already elapsed. Owner calls have a two-second timeout and the whole proof preparation has a five-second budget, matching the existing engineering targets above; unavailable or over-budget proof fails closed.

S02's `POST /internal/v1/workloads/{id}:receipt` accepts `purpose`, `requested_version`, optional `known_revision`, and `operation_deadline`. It returns the common signed owner receipt with `issuer_service=S02`, `audience_service=S06`, `resource_type=agent_workload`, `resource_id` equal to the workload ID, `resource_version` equal to the current active version ID, `resource_revision` and `authority_epoch` equal to the profile revision, and `content_digest` equal to the active immutable version's manifest digest. S02 issues an eligible receipt only when the requested version is the current active version and its parent service is active. A supplied known revision must match. The owner receipt's `key_id` selects an explicitly configured S02 verification key; the S02 private signing key is not shared with S01 or S06.

Owner receipt protocol v1 uses RS256 (`SHA256withRSA`) over UTF-8 RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme bytes of the receipt object with only `signature` omitted; `key_id` is included in the signed object. `signature` is unpadded base64url. RSA keys are at least 2048 bits. S06 verifies issuer, audience, tenant/environment, original actor, exact resource type/ID/version, purpose, eligibility, key allowlist, signature and validity interval. `issued_at` must not be in the future; `valid_until` must be after the check time, no more than five seconds after `issued_at`, and no later than the operation deadline. Owner receipts are snapshots, not continuing authorization.

S04's `POST /internal/v1/qualifications/{id}:topology-summary` accepts the expected digest/revision, purpose and cutoff and returns a point-in-time qualification result plus a closed summary containing only evidence ID, source class/reference, sequence, observed time, kind, qualification state/revision and payload digest. It does not disclose payload bytes or reviewer free text. `usable_as_evidence` means only that the retained `QUALIFIED` record passed the digest, binding, cutoff and replay/sequence-conflict checks. The summary preserves `observed_at`; S04 has no evidence-expiration field or freshness policy, so `QUALIFIED` or current record state is not freshness certification. Old evidence can remain eligible when the other bindings and cutoff checks pass. If a decision depends on freshness and the applicable query policy supplies no threshold, the result must disclose the limitation and be incomplete or indeterminate rather than infer freshness. The caller must use the exact purpose matching its S01 delegation. This response is an authenticated S04 owner API result, not an S02 ownership receipt.

### S06 asynchronous graph selection and difference classification

The canonical `POST /v1/graphs/subgraphs:select` (`canonical_028`) and `POST /v1/graphs/differences:classify` (`canonical_027`) request schemas, required fields and paths are unchanged. A `202` `CommandReceipt` is admission-only: `status=PENDING` and `domain_completion=false`. The committed command starts at `ACCEPTED`; its same-origin `status_resource` is read with authenticated `GET /v1/commands/{id}`. The current wire projection contains `command_id`, `status`, `terminal` and `domain_completion`; only a `SUCCEEDED` command also contains `advisory_result`. In the current worker, the ordinary path is `ACCEPTED` → `RUNNING` → `SUCCEEDED`, with retry path `RUNNING` → `RETRY_SCHEDULED` → `RUNNING` and terminal `FAILED` after five attempts or a non-retryable graph-binding failure. Retries are delayed by 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds before the remaining attempts. The persisted lifecycle schema also permits `WAITING_FOR_DEPENDENCY`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED` and `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`; the current S06 worker does not transition commands to those states. If present, `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` is nonterminal pending reconciliation. `terminal=true` only for `SUCCEEDED`, `REJECTED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED`; `domain_completion=true` only for `SUCCEEDED`. Results are advisory only.

The selection domain revision target is keyed by `service_id` and `query_id`. Graph, catalog, case and evidence context can therefore change while advancing `expected_revision` for that same target; the exact changing context is retained in the command's source binding. The difference-classification target is keyed by `service_id` and `authorized_graph_revision_id`. Current graph, retained selection, case and evidence context is retained in the source binding, so a subsequent comparison against that authorized graph advances its `expected_revision`.

For selection, `query_id` is the versioned allowlist ID `action-relevant-dependencies.v1` or `action-relevant-dependencies.v2`. v1 preserves the bounded dependency/failure-domain selection; v2 additionally selects accepted reachable `READS_FROM` edges from represented `service` and `agent_workload` nodes to `shared_resource` nodes. The exact query ID is retained with the selection and reused during difference recomputation. `query_parameters_ref` is an opaque S02 catalog revision reference shaped exactly as `catalog://service/{service_id}/revision/{resource_version}`, where `resource_version` is a positive decimal revision token; it is not an arbitrary URL and is never dereferenced as one. `query_parameters_digest` must equal the S02 owner receipt's content digest for that exact immutable service ID, resource version and resource revision. S06 first obtains the current signed S02 receipt (`resource_type=service`) using `POST /internal/v1/services/{id}:receipt`, with purpose, requested version, optional known revision and operation deadline. It then reads only `GET /internal/v1/services/{id}/revisions/{version}` using the same resource- and purpose-scoped S01 delegation. The read returns `resource_version`, `content_digest` and the retained exact `snapshot`; S06 verifies the response version and service ID, canonicalizes the snapshot with RFC 8785 JCS, and verifies its SHA-256 against both the read response and signed receipt before consuming any fields. S06 does not fetch the opaque reference as a URL and does not fall back to mutable current metadata.

Each newly registered S02 service revision snapshots its sorted `owned_agent_workload_ids` membership. Registering an agent workload bound to a service appends a new service revision and snapshot in the same transaction. Revisions are append-only and existing services/revisions are not backfilled with fabricated snapshots. A legacy snapshot without `owned_agent_workload_ids` has unknown membership, not an empty set; an absent exact revision is unavailable. S06 maps the service to workload roots only from this exact verified snapshot, intersects those IDs with `agent_workload` nodes in the exact accepted S06 graph revision, and traverses only existing accepted S06 edges. Catalog membership never materializes graph nodes or dependency edges. Missing revision or membership yields explicit incomplete/indeterminate coverage, not a mutable read or inferred relationship.

Both operations check `case_id` through a current S07 `assessment_case` receipt. The S07 receipt's `purpose` is the case's business purpose (for example, `incident`); the operation purpose is bound and authorized by the S01 delegation exchanged for that exact case ID and is not required to equal the receipt's business purpose. S04 supplies only bounded qualification summaries with observed-time metadata and no evidence payload or reviewer text; those summaries do not certify freshness.

The persisted selection result retains an internal `s04_summary_coverage` object; classification retains the corresponding maps as `authorized_evidence_coverage` and `current_evidence_coverage`. The command-status wire projection does not expose these objects or the underlying summaries. Each coverage object records `graph_edge_count`, `verified_summary_count`, `unverified_summary_count`, `summaries` (at most ten verified summaries), `truncated`, `complete` and bounded `reason_codes`. The reason-code vocabulary is `SUMMARY_INVALIDATED`, `SUMMARY_STALE`, `SUMMARY_UNAVAILABLE`, `SUMMARY_LIMIT_REACHED` and `SUMMARY_COVERAGE_GAP`. The public `advisory_result` is a smaller projection: selection returns `kind`, `completeness`, `result_digest`, `summary`, `advisory_only`, at most 1,000 `{node_type,node_id}` nodes, at most 5,000 existing `{source_type,source_id,relationship_type,target_type,target_id}` edges and the fixed unsupported-domain labels; classification returns the common fields plus `classification`, `selection_digest`, at most 100 `changes` and the fixed unsupported-domain labels. A change contains `change_type` and either `before` or `after` edge data, or both for a target change.

`TopologyController.commandStatus` currently returns the compact wire map above. The `TopologyResponses.CommandStatus` record declares a different set of fields and is not used by that handler; the contract documents the handler's actual response rather than treating the record as the wire shape.

The user-approved action-relevant selection scope spans bounded dependencies, failure domains, SLOs, journeys, customer segments, impacts, controls, fallbacks and recovery paths. Query v1 selects service-rooted workload dependencies and bounded represented region, cloud-resource, identity, model, and failure-domain context; query v2 adds accepted `READS_FROM` relationships from reachable `Service`/`ApplicationComponent` roles to the represented `DataStore` role. Both remain incomplete framework coverage. The canonical graph schema has 58 node labels and 55 relationship types across Evidence and tenancy, Infrastructure and location, Service and runtime, Identity ownership and control, AI and data, External context, Customer and business, Recovery and action, Decision provenance, and Collaborative agents. The selected failure-domain relation is `MEMBER_OF_FAILURE_DOMAIN`; legacy `IN_FAILURE_DOMAIN` proposals are normalized to that canonical relation. This slice does not claim coverage of labels such as `EvidenceEvent`, `EvidenceQualification`, `SLO`, `Control`, `CustomerJourney`, `CustomerSegment`, `Impact`, `RecoveryPath`, `Authorization`, or `DecisionEnvelope`, or relationships such as `ROUTES_TO`, `CALLS`, `FALLBACK_TO`, `PROTECTED_BY`, `SUPPORTS_STEP`, `SERVES_SEGMENT`, `GOVERNED_BY_SLO`, `AFFECTS`, `FAILURE_PROPAGATES_TO`, and `USES_RECOVERY_PATH`. Neither query result must be presented as complete UACRF graph selection. If freshness is material but the policy has no threshold, selection/classification is incomplete or indeterminate.

S01's closed owner-delegation map for these reads is limited to S02 plus `service` for `catalog.service.read`, S04 plus `evidence_event` for `evidence.topology.summary`, and S07 plus `assessment_case` for `assessment.case.read`. The only operation purpose IDs are `topology.graph.subgraph-selection` and `topology.graph.difference-classification`; each S04/S07 read uses the matching ID. S01 rechecks `topology.graph.read` for these operations and derives the least-privilege owner capability from the authenticated operation and resource type; callers cannot supply a capability or choose an arbitrary owner. S02's catalog receipt is bound to the exact immutable revision, S04 returns only the bounded summary, and S07 returns only current case state.

These are direct S06 operations and owner reads; S06 domain events remain S06-produced and are not routed through S03. Both operations' outputs, including `advisory_result`, are advisory-only: they do not create or revise graph edges, promote a graph, authorize execution or establish recovery. The private schema reference defines 58 node labels and 55 relationship types; v1 covers service-rooted workload dependencies and bounded represented region, cloud-resource, identity, model and failure-domain context, while v2 additionally selects reachable accepted `READS_FROM` edges to represented shared resources. The canonical `MEMBER_OF_FAILURE_DOMAIN` relation is used for new accepted graph revisions; legacy `IN_FAILURE_DOMAIN` proposals are normalized, and retained legacy graph edges are interpreted equivalently during selection and classification. v2 classifies read additions as `data_read_added`, removals as `data_read_removed`, and exact same-source target replacements as `data_read_target_changed`; mixed or unpaired read deltas are indeterminate. Relationship properties such as `observed_at`, `valid_to` or `expires_at` do not establish freshness. The S02 membership snapshot bridges the selection request's `service_id` to candidate `agent_workload` roots without equating a catalog membership with a graph relationship. Difference classification does not carry a new S02 service-revision reference; its `selection_digest` must resolve to the retained S06 selection for the same service and authorized graph revision. Missing or mismatched selection context is rejected. Supported deltas are classified only within that retained scope, while broader ontology and freshness coverage remain incomplete or indeterminate.

S06 retains the typed proposal, the S02 proof for each endpoint, and the S04 summary/digest/revision. Proposal reads expose the bounded summary and references, never raw evidence bytes. Before any review decision and before reporting a graph current-use receipt, S06 repeats the S02 and S04 calls with fresh resource-scoped delegations. A changed endpoint version/revision/digest, expired or ineligible owner receipt, changed evidence revision/digest, non-qualified state, or S04 conflict blocks current use; it does not rewrite or revoke an accepted graph revision. Graph current-use checks cover only evidence-backed proposals in the requested graph. A fresh proposal and independent review, followed by accepting a new graph revision, is required to restore current-use eligibility. The graph digest binds edge provenance (`proposal_id`) so a newly reviewed support proposal creates a distinct immutable revision even if the typed edge is unchanged. No cache or event is treated as current proof.

The implemented S07 learning-review receipt is an exception to the target protocol above: it is an internal, unsigned point-in-time verification response, not a signed owner receipt or adoption authorization. It requires global-scope `assessment.learning.read` and `UACRF_ASSESSMENT_LEARNING_REVIEW_ENABLED=true`, and is not routed through EDGE01 or consumed by S10. S07 verifies an approved review for the proposal revision, compares its SHA-256 digest with `owned_digest` in exactly one retained review outbox event, and revalidates supporting incident evaluation evidence with current owners. The digest uses the current Jackson serialization rather than RFC 8785, and the check does not verify the whole outbox payload digest; serialization changes or removal of the event can fail closed. Owner checks are sequential, and later changes can invalidate the result.

The implemented S01 objective-review authority check takes `tenant_id`, `environment_id`
and `reviewer_id`. A directly authenticated SYSTEM caller requires a current
global `objectives.review-authority.check` membership; that capability cannot be
delegated. In one database statement S01 checks current caller and reviewer
status, trusted identity providers and global memberships. An authorized result
contains the scope, reviewer, status and database check time; denials do not
disclose whether the reviewer exists. It is unsigned, has no authority epoch or
validity interval, and does not bind an objective, decision or operation. It is
not the target access receipt or an activation authorization, and subsequent
revocation may invalidate it. It is not routed by EDGE01. With the optional
`UACRF_OBJECTIVES_REVIEWER_CHECK_ENABLED` gate enabled, S05 verifies the
retained reviewer's current authority through this response before activation,
using direct SYSTEM credentials. It rejects unavailable, malformed, mismatched
or stale responses, but does not check the activator, bind the response to an
objective or prevent revocation between the check and commit. With the gate
disabled, activation performs no reviewer recheck.
The development Keycloak realm defines the confidential
`uacrf-s05-objective-reviewer` client and its SYSTEM service-account subject
with the S01 context/audience scope. Provisioning it does not admit the
principal into S01 or grant `objectives.review-authority.check`; both remain
explicit identity administration.

For both review approval and receipt issuance, S07 additionally checks typed proposal content against the separately stored incident and evidence references, recomputes its digest and compares it with `owned_digest` in exactly one retained proposal event. Inconsistency or a missing event fails closed. This proposal digest also uses current Jackson serialization and is not a versioned canonical scheme; the check does not authenticate the event or verify its full payload.

S07 also rechecks the proposal's expected incident revision and requires its evidence to be a subset of the incident's evidence, even when the supporting evaluations contain additional verified measurements. A previously retained approval remains historical and readable, but does not produce a current verification response if this binding is inconsistent.

Adaptation draft creation requires global-scope `assessment.learning.write` and `assessment.learning.read`, both the adaptation and learning-review feature flags, and a fresh successful learning-review check. The retained learning digests and check time are historical evidence only. Target owner, controlled version, proposed reviewer, tests and rollback criteria are caller declarations, not owner-verified or independently approved; no owner workflow is contacted. Reads require global-scope `assessment.learning.read`, even when creation is disabled. Listing requires `learning_record_id`, uses an ID cursor and `limit` 1–100 (default 50); separate pages are not a snapshot. Creation requires `Idempotency-Key`, scoped to tenant, environment and actor; same-content retries return the retained draft without rechecking owners, while changed content conflicts. Replays still require the feature flags and capabilities and are not current approval. Matching uses a hash of the current Jackson serialization and may conflict after serialization changes. The transactional local outbox event is unpublished and is not an execution permit. No EDGE01 route or target-owner submission exists.

Owner checks have a proposed two-second per-call timeout and a five-second overall current-context preparation budget under the nominal profile. Slow actual artifact verification can use durable asynchronous commands instead of holding transactions open. These are engineering targets, not measured service performance. Safe read retries must stay within the original operation deadline. Permission denied, stale revision, expired source and unavailable owner remain distinct internal outcomes.

## Event protocol and topics

`contracts/event-envelope.schema.json` defines a closed envelope. Payload schemas are registered by event type/version and validated locally; the consumer must not fetch an arbitrary schema URL supplied by an event. `EVENT_CATALOG.json` assigns producer, intended consumers, partition key, meaning and payload requirements.

For new cross-service messages, use RFC 8785 canonical JSON and SHA-256 over the canonical payload bytes. Restrict numeric payload values to bounded integers; exact decimal money, quantities and rates use validated strings. Do not pass arbitrary-precision source numbers through a JavaScript double. Preserve Unicode strings and original signed artifact bytes. Existing PostgreSQL canonical text and source-file hashes retain their original protocol and are wrapped with an explicit legacy digest scheme rather than silently recanonicalized.

Each topic partition key is `tenant_id|environment_id|aggregate_type|aggregate_id`, with reserved-delimiter escaping defined by the transport library. Topic ACLs allow the owning service to produce its facts. A consumer cannot publish another owner's event name. Inbox identity is producer/event ID; aggregate sequence and payload digest detect ordering gaps and conflicting duplicates.

## Common durable command lifecycle

A command begins ACCEPTED after local commit. A leased worker can move it to RUNNING, WAITING_FOR_DEPENDENCY or RETRY_SCHEDULED. Terminal states are SUCCEEDED, REJECTED, FAILED, CANCELLED and EXPIRED. OUTCOME_UNKNOWN is a retained non-success state for external ambiguity and requires reconciliation before a justified terminal transition. Existing public status vocabulary is mapped through an explicit compatibility serializer; do not silently change a closed legacy enum.

A lease has owner, token, generation and expiry. Prepare/finish calls include the lease token and expected aggregate revision. Source work occurs outside database locks. Completion compares current source receipts, authority, cancellation generation and lease before committing result, audit and outbox atomically. An old worker can retain a permitted late observation, but cannot overwrite the current decision.

## Execution and authorization lifecycle

An authorization is ISSUED, RESERVED, CONSUMED, REVOKED or EXPIRED. Reservation binds one attempt. A DISPATCHING execution attempt is written before a provider request. Provider observation can establish SUCCEEDED, FAILED, CANCEL_REQUESTED or OUTCOME_UNKNOWN; no implicit retry follows Unknown. A completed provider effect may lead to VALIDATION_PENDING and then VALIDATED, VALIDATION_FAILED or VALIDATION_UNKNOWN. Only an approved independent validation can establish the configured recovered outcome.

A compensation workflow is separately authorized and retains its own attempts. It can succeed, fail or remain unknown. Do not model compensation success as deletion of earlier effects. Human intervention and a reconciliation record are legitimate outcomes when an external action cannot be safely reversed.

## Target additions for prediction and learning

S07 will add versioned feature-set, model-card, model-evaluation, forecast and incident-learning records. Proposed internal endpoints are `POST /internal/v1/model-evaluations`, `POST /internal/v1/forecasts`, `GET /internal/v1/forecasts/{id}`, `POST /internal/v1/learning-records` and `POST /internal/v1/adaptation-proposals`. Their public exposure, if needed, receives separately published contracts; these are not secretly appended to the canonical count.

A forecast pins model/version, feature-set digest, training/evaluation lineage, forecast time/horizon, uncertainty and mode. An adaptation proposal identifies owner, affected controlled version, reason, required tests, independent reviewer and rollback criteria. No endpoint can directly promote an unreviewed model or alter production action policy from a model response.

## Provider and artifact ports

Collection ports expose capability discovery, inventory pages, typed observation batches, configuration snapshots and source-definition verification. Execution ports expose non-mutating translation, current precondition read, bounded dispatch, operation-status reconciliation, cancellation and separately authorized compensation. An Unsupported result names the unavailable capability and cannot satisfy a success acceptance test.

Artifacts use owner-local `stage`, `commit`, `verify` and `open-authorized-stream` ports. Immutable metadata includes digest, byte length, media type, classification, purpose and retention policy. A model or user reference never becomes an arbitrary outbound fetch. Native provider adapters declare their concrete credential scope, network targets, request-id and conditional-update behavior in their conformance record.
